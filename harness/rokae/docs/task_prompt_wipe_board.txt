# Task prompt

Read docs/agent_prompt.txt first. 

1. Task description

- Goal (object/workspace and desired result): Wipe the marker marks from the whiteboard.
- Initial state (tool, grasp and starting assumptions): The eraser is already held in the gripper when the task starts.
- Completion (observable success criteria): All marker marks are gone. 


2. Action requirements

- Observations (fields needed to choose actions and check completion)

  python get_observation.py --observations force tactile_tip_world.wrench eef_pose joint_angles left_wrist.rgb right_wrist.rgb head.rgb --wrench tcp --tactile-reference connector

  (1). Use robot force[0:3] for force feedback. However, force and torque estimation can have errors, thus reading in the force and torque observation does not suggest contact. Always judge contact together with the vision if needed.
  (2). For torque feedback, use only tactile_tip_world.wrench with frame=world and reference=connector (cable tip). However, torque control should not be needed most of the time in this task as the robot is compliant.

- Sequence (required order of actions): 
  (0). Calibrate tactile and force observations at task start with the current cable grasp still and clear of socket contact. Repeat after grasp changes, never under external contact.
  (1) Forward to make contact with the board and maintain the desired force.
  (2) After making contact, maintaining contact while sliding parallel to the board. You can plan the wiping strategy by yourself if some markers are not cleaned as expected, but the recommended pattern is scanning horizontally.

- Contact target (desired contact, force or other physical conditions): Establish meaningful contact force near the active soft threshold. Reset the soft limit to 10N and the control goal through motion command should be 10N as well.

- Motion style (preferred movement pattern and action size): Before contact, you can command larger steps from 5cm to 8cm, given that the force control is designed safe thus you can be confident. During wiping, use long, purposeful wiping strokes where the marked area calls for them, within the system's motion limits. Command a target moderately beyond the board surface to maintain contact under impedance control, adjusting from visual and force feedback. Otherwise the eraser may lift off the surface if it is inclined.

- Restrictions (task-specific actions to avoid): Keep the eraser grasped; gripper commands should not be needed.


3. Task-specific notes

- Geometry (working pose, coordinate assumptions and object relationships): For the wiping orientation, assume the wrist camera's forward axis (camera Z) aligns with world X, the first delta translation component. In the wrist image, positive world Y points left and positive world Z points up.

- Visibility (occlusions, useful views and ways to improve observation): The wrist camera is above the wrist, so the eraser may obscure the marked area; start from the bottom. If the next action or completion is unclear, retreat far enough within the motion limits to widen the wrist camera's view. Use the head view for complementary information. The right camera helps to provide auxiliary view when the left camera is blocked by the gripper.

- Feedback interpretation (task-specific ambiguities): An estimated force before reaching the board may be a false contact indication. Establish actual board contact using images and force feedback together before sliding.

- Decision rules (observation-to-action examples):

  - Marker to the right of the eraser: move right in the image, corresponding to negative world Y.
  - Contact force approaching the active soft threshold: retreat slightly, since excessive friction may prevent sliding.
  - Contact force below/over the control target: advance/withdraw slightly to maintain contact. This acts as a simple force controller.

- important note:

  - the finger is grasping the top part of the eraser, thus do not think that the marker will be erased as long as the bottom part of the eraser covers the marker. use the finger tip location with half of the eraser area as an assumed region where desired force are applied and thus the marker is cleared.
